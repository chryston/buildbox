# Floorplanner patterns worth adapting

Research for [BuildBox issue #10](https://github.com/chryston/buildbox/issues/10), retrieved 2026-10-07. This compares Floorplanner's public product and documentation with the destination in [issue #9](https://github.com/chryston/buildbox/issues/9). It does not infer private implementation details. Unless marked **Recommendation**, statements below are observations from first-party sources.

## Decision summary

Use Floorplanner as evidence for a **single project model with multiple floors and multiple projections of shared data**, for direct manipulation of measured geometry, and for derived exports/quantities. Do not treat Floorplanner's per-floor design variants as BuildBox renovation phases or drawing layers. BuildBox should independently model phase and layer as orthogonal element attributes, keep height/elevation in its 2D-authored model, and prioritize a portable local project file plus deterministic 2D/quantity exports. Defer photorealistic 3D, cloud sharing, large catalogs, CAD/scan interchange, and design variants.

## Capability comparison

| Area | Observed Floorplanner pattern | BuildBox decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unified projects and views | A project represents one property and contains floors and per-floor designs. The same design switches between 2D and 3D; saved cameras are additional views, not independent geometry ([editor manual, pp. 3, 14–17, 22–23](https://fpcdn.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/static/brochures/Floorplanner-editor-manual-04-2025.pdf)). The public API likewise nests floors and designs under a project ([OpenAPI: project creation and floor/design endpoints](https://site.floorplanner.com/openapi.json)). | **Adapt now:** one canonical BuildBox project owns floors, plan geometry, cabinets/items, annotations, phases, layers, and derived outputs. Floor-plan and cabinet/item screens should be projections of that data, never separately saved projects. |
| Floors, designs, layers, phases | Floors are ordered/named and have a height; each floor can have multiple designs for alternative layouts or interior styles ([manual, pp. 22–23](https://fpcdn.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/static/brochures/Floorplanner-editor-manual-04-2025.pdf)). Current tiers advertise three floors/three designs for free projects and up to seven floors/ten designs per floor after upgrade ([project levels](https://floorplanner.com/project-levels)). The reviewed manual, product pages, and API do not document construction phases or a general drawing/discipline layer model. | **Adapt floors only. Do not copy designs as phases.** Model `floor`, semantic `phase` (existing/demolition/proposed), and visibility/discipline `layer` independently. Defer alternative-design branching until a real comparison workflow exists. |
| Walls and curved geometry | Users can draw rooms or individual walls, enter exact lengths, snap, split segments, drag corners, angle walls, and curve a selected wall segment. Enclosed walls produce room surfaces ([manual, pp. 26–30](https://fpcdn.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/static/brochures/Floorplanner-editor-manual-04-2025.pdf)). Surface edges can also be reshaped or curved ([manual, p. 34](https://fpcdn.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/static/brochures/Floorplanner-editor-manual-04-2025.pdf)). | **Adapt now:** direct manipulation plus numeric entry, snapping, split/join, and a geometry model composed of explicit straight and circular-arc segments. Keep room/finish-zone boundaries derived from or explicitly associated with that geometry; do not special-case a visually curved wall. |
| Heights, elevations, and 3D | Walls expose thickness, height, and raise; wall-corner heights create slopes. Surfaces can have whole-shape or per-corner elevation, and objects have dimensions/vertical position ([manual, pp. 7, 31, 34–36, 51](https://fpcdn.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/static/brochures/Floorplanner-editor-manual-04-2025.pdf)). Floorplanner renders the model as dollhouse/first-person views and offers cameras, lighting, high-resolution renders, and VR tours ([manual, pp. 14–21](https://fpcdn.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/static/brochures/Floorplanner-editor-manual-04-2025.pdf)). | **Adapt the data, defer the spectacle:** retain floor elevation, element base elevation, height, and relevant profile data in a 3D-aware 2D model. Defer a full 3D renderer, cameras, lighting, scenery, photorealistic exports, VR, and GLB until a later 3D consumer justifies them. |
| Objects and comments | Floorplanner has a searchable furniture/object library; selected objects can be moved, resized, rotated, mirrored, raised, and custom-labeled. Information tools include room types, text, symbols, lines, and dimensions ([manual, pp. 4–9, 40–51](https://fpcdn.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/static/brochures/Floorplanner-editor-manual-04-2025.pdf)). No threaded or spatial review-comment workflow is documented in the current manual or public API. The API's `comment` on a project permission is expressly a reason for granting permission, not an in-plan discussion ([OpenAPI: project permissions](https://site.floorplanner.com/openapi.json)). | **Adapt now:** typed object instances with stable IDs, transform/size/elevation, editable properties, and selection-driven inspection. Design BuildBox comments independently as stable entity- or coordinate-anchored records; do not confuse labels with comments. Defer a huge branded catalog and real-time collaborative threads. |
| Save, history, and import | The editor exposes save/undo/redo, design duplication, and restore of an older design version, but public docs do not specify autosave, retention, merge, or conflict guarantees ([manual, pp. 3–4, 22](https://fpcdn.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/static/brochures/Floorplanner-editor-manual-04-2025.pdf)). End-user tracing accepts PNG/JPG/PDF backgrounds ([manual, pp. 24–25](https://fpcdn.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/static/brochures/Floorplanner-editor-manual-04-2025.pdf)). API surfaces additionally accept FML JSON/XML and Apple Roomplan JSON ([OpenAPI: import endpoints](https://site.floorplanner.com/openapi.json)). | **Adapt now:** explicit, atomic local save; schema-versioned portable JSON; undo/redo; import validation and clear failure reporting. Background-image tracing may follow. Defer history retention, CAD/Roomplan import, merge/conflict handling, and cloud sync. |
| Export | The editor documents 2D and 3D JPG/PNG/PDF exports ([manual, pp. 57–58](https://fpcdn.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/static/brochures/Floorplanner-editor-manual-04-2025.pdf)). Upgraded projects add FML/DXF and share links ([project levels](https://floorplanner.com/project-levels)); API export also lists SVG and a GLB mesh option ([OpenAPI: project export](https://site.floorplanner.com/openapi.json)). FML is a JSON format spanning walls, openings, labels, areas, and furniture ([FML overview](https://floorplanner.com/fml)). Availability therefore varies by surface/tier. | **Adapt the boundary, not Floorplanner's format:** make the native project file lossless and documented; make PNG/SVG/PDF and quantity outputs deterministic derivatives. Defer DXF/GLB and FML compatibility until an interoperability requirement and license review exist. |
| Quantities | The project menu can list and download items/materials used in a plan ([manual, p. 22](https://fpcdn.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/static/brochures/Floorplanner-editor-manual-04-2025.pdf)). API endpoints return area measurements by project/floor/design and product lists as CSV/JSON ([OpenAPI: measurements and products endpoints](https://site.floorplanner.com/openapi.json)). Enterprise integrations can show a shopping list updated while designing ([enterprise product page](https://floorplanner.com/enterprise)). | **Adapt now:** derive floor/finish-zone areas, wall lengths, and placed-item counts from canonical geometry, grouped by floor/phase/layer with source IDs. Never store editable totals beside source geometry. Defer pricing, procurement, retailer catalogs, and a generalized bill of materials. |
| Sharing | Upgraded projects can publish links and embedded interactive viewers; VR tours are browser links ([project levels](https://floorplanner.com/project-levels), [manual, p. 21](https://fpcdn.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/static/brochures/Floorplanner-editor-manual-04-2025.pdf)). The API distinguishes public/private projects, permissions, publish/unpublish, and a decorate-only share link ([OpenAPI](https://site.floorplanner.com/openapi.json)). Team plans add users, roles, shared assets, and reporting, but do not establish real-time co-editing ([team product page](https://floorplanner.com/team)). | **Defer cloud sharing:** first share a portable project file or read-only export. Keep persistence and permissions behind backend-ready boundaries, but do not select auth/sync or expose public links in this static/local-first phase. Later roles should be capability-based (view/comment/edit), with explicit publish/revoke and private-by-default behavior. |

## Patterns to adopt

1. **One source of truth, several views.** Persist domain objects once; derive floor-plan, cabinet/item, quantity, and future 3D views.
2. **A visible hierarchy and context.** Always expose active project/floor and view filters; make phase/layer visibility explicit rather than hiding it inside variants.
3. **Progressive geometry editing.** Start with direct manipulation and snapping, then expose exact numeric properties in one consistent inspector.
4. **3D-ready coordinates without a 3D dependency.** Define elevation/height and arc semantics now so later rendering does not require migration.
5. **Derived measurements and exports.** Quantities and presentation files are reproducible outputs of the canonical model.
6. **Portable ownership.** A versioned local project export is the recovery and handoff boundary before any backend exists.

## Patterns to defer or reject

- Defer photorealistic rendering, 3D tours, cameras, lighting/scenery, and mesh export.
- Defer catalogs, room styles, auto-layout, retail shopping lists, white labeling, SSO, and usage-credit/project-tier mechanics.
- Defer alternative design branches; they are not substitutes for renovation phase or discipline layer.
- Defer hosted permissions, public embeds, and concurrent editing. Do not imply collaboration merely because several users can access an account.
- Reject cloud-only persistence and export gating: they conflict with BuildBox's local-first destination.
- Reject copying Floorplanner's names, information architecture, screen composition, icons, copy, FML schema, catalog data, or styling merely to appear “Floorplanner-like.”

## Legal and product-risk boundary

This is product guidance, not legal advice.

- Floorplanner's terms retain ownership of its software, databases, websites, content, patents, copyrights, know-how, and trade secrets; the customer receives a non-transferable use license, not reuse rights ([terms, clauses 2.2 and 5.1](https://floorplanner.com/terms)). Do not copy source, assets, screenshots, text, icons, templates, model/catalog data, or branded visual identity.
- Its current OpenAPI document identifies its license as “Copyright Floorplanner,” not an open-source license ([OpenAPI](https://site.floorplanner.com/openapi.json)). Public readability is not permission to clone the API or internal schema.
- FML has a separate license. It permits copying/modification/distribution only with file attribution, the full notice, copyright tags, modification notices, same-license distribution, and a grant back to Floorplanner for modifications ([FML License Notice, version 1, 2024](https://cdn.floorplanner.com/uploads/licenses/fml-license-v01c-04102024.pdf)). BuildBox should use an independently designed native schema unless deliberate FML interoperability outweighs those obligations.
- Floorplanner's free-user terms grant Floorplanner a perpetual, transferable right to publish, change, use, and sublicense free users' plans, while paid-customer terms separately assign ownership of customer projects/work product ([terms, clauses 5.2 and 12.3](https://floorplanner.com/terms)). BuildBox should instead state a clear, minimal user-content license consistent with local ownership and private-by-default behavior.
- General product ideas and methods are distinct from their original expression: the U.S. Copyright Office says copyright does not protect ideas, procedures, processes, systems, or methods, while original text, graphics, and illustrations may be protected ([Circular 33, pp. 1–3](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf)). The safe adaptation is independent implementation of general capabilities, with BuildBox terminology, workflows, UI, assets, tests, and schema. Trademark, trade-dress, patent, contract, and jurisdiction-specific questions still require counsel before a close clone.
- Literal copying also creates product debt even where legally permissible: Floorplanner's hierarchy serves interior design, monetized rendering, and retail catalogs. Copying it would blur BuildBox's renovation phases/layers, couple persistence to hosted services, and import irrelevant complexity.

## Recommendation for the wayfinder

Specify and sequence the Floorplanner-inspired core as: **canonical project → floors → line/arc geometry and odd-shaped zones/items → height/elevation → orthogonal phase and layer attributes → annotations/comments → derived quantities → local project and 2D exports**. Treat cabinet/item and floor-plan experiences as coordinated views throughout. Leave design variants, hosted sharing/collaboration, broad import/interchange, and 3D presentation to later issues with explicit demand.
